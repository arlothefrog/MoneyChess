MONEY CHESS

Rules, mechanics & implementation specification â€” for human players and AI coding agents.

This document is the authoritative specification of money-chess.html. It describes how the game actually behaves (read from the source code), notes where the behaviour differs from stated intent, and lists the key functions an agent will need when modifying the game.

How to run the game: open money-chess.html in any modern browser. It is a single self-contained HTML file (no build step, no dependencies). Online play loads PeerJS from a CDN at runtime and therefore needs internet access for that feature only. There is also a Readme.html (this file) and AGENTS.md.

1. Starting a game (the menu)

Before any game, the page shows a menu overlay with three buttons:

  • Start Game â€” closes the menu and begins a fresh, randomly seeded game.
  • Sandbox â€” starts a normal local game (vs AI if enabled) with the God Mode button available (see Â§2). Online play buttons are hidden for the whole session; a normal Start Game restores them.
  • Settings â€” Sound volume slider (0–100%, saved between sessions) plus on/off switches for the Music loop and the Turn notifier chime, and the AI difficulty slider (1–11, see §12). (Board size, 8x8/Chess 960, is picked via the in-game control bar.) While in a game, a Settings button at the top-right of the screen opens the same volume slider, the same toggles, the same AI difficulty slider, and a Quit to menu option. A calm synthesized piano loop plays the whole time the game is open (menu and play) and follows the same volume slider.

3. Objective & endings

  • Players are White (bottom) and Black (top). White moves first.
  • Win: capture the enemy king. This is a king-capture variant â€” there is no checkmate rule. The game warns with "Check!" but does not force you to resolve it; if you leave your king in danger your opponent simply captures it. The king may also move into check (onto an attacked square) — it is then simply captured. Only castling is restricted: the king may not castle out of, through, or into an attacked square. So a "checkmated" or "stalemated" player still has legal moves and loses the king next turn.
  • Draw: if the side to move has no legal moves at all (every piece asleep, frozen or blocked), the game ends early with "Stalemate â€” Draw".
  • The status bar reports "White/Black wins (king captured)" or "Stalemate â€” Draw".

4. Turn structure

Each turn you get, in any order: one piece action plus one optional free spell.

  1. Move a piece (a normal chess move) â€” you may still cast a spell afterwards.
  2. Buy & place from the shop â€” this ends your turn (it is intended to be "your move").
  3. Cast one spell â€” a free action (per turn), no matter when; after casting you may still move.

Notes:

  • Casting a spell sets "spell used this turn"; a second spell is only possible via the Freecast spell or a "2x"/"RND" free follow-up.
  • Buying is blocked after casting a spell in the same turn; a move also ends the turn outright, so move-then-buy is not possible.
  • The Echo spell grants an extra turn (no captures during it).
  • Tax Gain grants +$3 and skips your move. With "2x" it forces a decision: take a second free Tax or press Skip Turn. With "RND" it banks the +$3 and offers one random spell immediately: cast it free (your turn then ends) or press Skip Turn â€” except Echo (its own extra turn keeps the turn with you) and Freecast (its granted free spell keeps the turn with you so it can actually be used).

    • 5. Money & economy

    • Each player starts with $10 (initialState()).
    • Capturing earns the mover floor(value/2) of the captured piece, where value is its shop price for shop pieces (rat $4→2, cat $6→3, shooter $7→3) and PIECE_VALUE otherwise (pawn 0, knight/bishop 1, rook 2, queen 4, Amazon 6).
    • Bounties add +$1; golden pieces +$1 per capture; platinum +$3 per capture; piratified pieces +$2 per capture; thieves take $1 from the opponent per capture.
    • Tax Gain (+$3 and skip turn) is the classic money "generation" spell; Sacrifice ($1) liquefies one of your non-king pieces into its purchase value.
    • Money is spent on shop pieces and on spell card costs (spells charge their printed price but do not consume your move).
    Design note: spell cards display a price (SPELLS[].cost: Tax Gain $0, Lightning $1, Snipe Pawn $2, Wall $1, Sleep $4, Bounty $0, Dispel $3, Echo $5, Push $3, Freecast $3, Confusion $4, Piratify $3, Thief $3, Curse $3, Shield $3, Swap $4, Sacrifice $1). Casting deducts this cost from the caster's money (castSpell). So spells are a "free action" (they do not use your move) but they still cost their printed price from your wallet, unless they are cast for free via Freecast or a "2x"/"RND" follow-up.

    8. Shop & placement

    • Three piece cards are always offered; under each card a dashed NEXT tile previews the exact next offering.
    • A purchase deducts money, enters placement mode, and you click an empty square on your side (White: lower half, Black: top half; the exact centre row of an odd board belongs to nobody). Placing ends your turn.
    • Prices: pawn $1, knight/bishop $3, rook $5, queen $9, camel $2, cricket $1, d-pawn $1, soldier $1, centaur $7, rat $4, cat $6, shooter $7, fake knight $4. Modifiers adjust price: pricier +$1, discount âˆ’$1 (min $0).
    • Shop modifiers: golden 4.95%, platinum 0.05%, pricier 2.5%, discount 2.5%, normal 90%. Golden pieces gain +$1 per capture (gold square glow); platinum +$3 per capture (silver glow). Discount/pricier affect price only.
    • The shop and the spell shop each refresh independently every 4 end-of-turn ticks (tickShopRefresh); the refresh bar shows progress. Buying a piece resets the shop timer; casting a spell resets the spell timer.
    • A bought piece is only replaced in its slot after it is placed (pendingRefillShopSlot).

    9. Board sizes, castling & Chess 960

    • Board sizes: 5, 8, 10, 12, 15 (+ Random at menu/game start). Square rendering shrinks automatically.
    • The standard back rank (makeBackRank) is built symmetrically: King at the centre file, Queen beside it, then Bishops/Knights/Rooks fanning out. Caveat: because the back rank is generated from the centre, only certain sizes place a Rook on an edge file. pseudoMoves requires an edge Rook to castle, so in practice castling works fully on the 8x8 board only (15x15 can in principle castle queen-side, since its file-0 square is a Rook; 5x5 / 10x10 / 12x12 have no edge Rooks at all). See Known Issues #1.
    • Chess 960 (RANDOM960): the king is placed on a random file and every other file gets a random piece from the roster (mirrored for both sides). Castling is disabled in 960 mode. The 960 back rank may include duplicate pieces and even pawns on the back rank (see Known Issues #2).

    10. Online multiplayer (PeerJS)

    • Two players, direct peer-to-peer, no server beyond PeerJS's broker. Host is authoritative and is assigned White or Black at random (coin flip); the Guest gets the other colour and sends only intent commands.
    • Both clients use the same SEED, so shop/spell/random outcomes replay identically. The host broadcasts full state snapshots after every mutation; the guest renders them and sends commands for its moves/buys/spells/targets.
    • Room codes: mc + 5 chars. The host's session is saved to localStorage so a reloaded tab can resume the same room/code; a disconnected guest retries for a 120s grace period.
    • If the host closes the room intentionally, the game ends and the room is closed for good.
    • Controls: Host Online (generates a code to share, then waits for the guest to join), Join (enter a code), Close Room.

    11. Seed & determinism

    • Every game begins with a random 32-bit SEED (generated by newSeed()) displayed at the top ("Seed â€¦"). It is shown but not editable.
    • All randomness (shop rolls, spell rolls, 960 ranks, confused piece moves, Lightning, AI dice, Random board size) flows through gRng(), a deterministic PRNG seeded by SEED and a counter. Two clients with the same seed+state reproduce identical games.

    12. AI opponent

    • The AI plays Black when "Play vs AI" is clicked before a game. Its strength is the AI difficulty slider (1–11, default 6) in both Settings panels, saved with the other settings (AI_LEVEL, moneyChessSettings.aiLevel) and applied from the AI's next decision.
    • Difficulty is a node budget, not a time limit (AI_LEVEL_NODES, aiLevelBudget): every minimax/quiesce call counts a node, so a level always plays the same way on any machine. Each level needs roughly half the effort of the next. Level 11 takes about 5 s a move on the reference machine (8x8 middlegames: 2–11 s depending on the position, depth 6–9), level 6 about 0.2 s (depth 3–6). Bigger boards get a budget scaled down by board area. Levels 1–3 also cap the depth (1/2/3), levels 1–4 blur the root scores (AI_LEVEL_NOISE, a deterministic hash, no RNG draw) so they make human-sized mistakes, and levels 1–2 skip the opponent model (below).
    • Thinking happens in a Web Worker, so a long search never freezes the page or the music. The AI engine is its own <script id="aiEngine"> block; aiWorkerSource() builds the worker from that text plus the main script's top-level functions (their source) and top-level data (serialised), and the worker runs aiDecide() on a copy of the state. If a worker cannot be created or fails, the same decision runs on the main thread. A decision that comes back after the game moved on (Reset, new game) is dropped; Reset also stops a busy worker (aiCancelThinking). The status line shows "AI (level N) thinking…" meanwhile.
    • Search: minimax with alpha-beta, iterative deepening, a transposition table (positionHash, 32-bit key verified by a second 32-bit hash; it covers the board and piece flags, side to move, castling, en passant, every effect with its remaining turns, no-capture counters, extra turns and both wallets), killer & history move ordering and late-move reductions. The root (aiRootSearch) stops deepening when the next iteration would not fit the budget and abandons one that overruns (SEARCH_ABORT), keeping the last finished depth (or a better move already proven in the cut-short iteration). Quiescence resolves captures, shots and promotions with delta pruning, and tries every evasion when in check.
    • Search nodes avoid allocation: cloneBoardRows shallow-copies only the rows a move touches and shares unchanged piece objects (pieces are never edited in place during the search), empty effect lists are shared, the side-to-move invariants (blocked set, king square, in-check flag) are computed once per allMoves, and the evaluation reuses module-level buffers instead of allocating per call (it runs at every quiescence leaf).
    • King-capture rules in the search: the search never lets a king step onto an attacked square or leave its king attacked (that just loses it), testing king moves with the king lifted off its square so it cannot hide behind itself on a checking line. A side with no such move is not drawn: under the live rules it must still move and lose its king, so the position is scored as lost (noMoveScore). Only a side with no legal move at all is a draw. Checkmate and stalemate are therefore both wins for the attacker.
    • The search follows the live rules for effects: Walls, Sleep, Shield and swap locks count down at the end of every turn exactly as expireEffectsAtTurnEnd() does (expireSnapEffects), and placements and Tax pass the turn the way placePiece/spellEndTurnSkip do. A confused piece's moves are a chance option worth their average (splitConfused, chanceValue), not a free choice; quiescence ignores its captures.
    • Opponent model (aiOppExtras, level 3+, AI_PARAMS.oppModel): on the human's first reply the search also tries a few shop drops (the two priciest affordable pieces on the squares that block a check or hit the most of our material, paid from the snapshot wallet) and the spells on the bar the human will see (Sleep, Confusion, Push, Snipe Pawn, Shield, Wall, Dispel) aimed at the AI pieces that threaten the most. The bar after an AI cast is the refilled one from state.nextSpells, and after an AI buy the shop slot shows state.nextShop[0].
    • One decision, compared at one depth (aiDecide): the plain move search sets the depth D; then targeted spells ("spell, then the best move", aiSpellValue), shop buys (each piece on its shortlisted squares, aiBestBuy/aiBestPlacement) and Tax Gain (aiTaxGainValue) are all searched to that same total depth, so their values compare. Each only has to beat the current bar (searched with it as alpha), and a two-ply-shallower probe skips the clearly worse ones (AI_PROBE_MARGIN). A buy or Tax ends the turn, so it must beat the better of the move and the spell turn by buyMargin/taxMargin; a spell must beat the move by spellMargin. The whole decision stops considering options at 2.5× the budget. After a spell the AI plays the move found with it when it is still legal; otherwise (a random spell changed the board, a 2x/RND free card appeared) it decides again.
    • Spells (aiSpellTargets): each spell offers its top three heuristic targets (aiSleepTargets, aiShieldTargets, aiBlockTargets, aiSwapTargets, aiMarkTargets, …); those above the spell's threshold are searched in heuristic order (2/4/8 searches at levels 1–3/4–6/7+). Lightning and Bounty have random effects and are scored by expectation less the money they cost. Echo is searched (its extra, capture-free turn is modelled). Freecast is not modelled. A free 2x/RND follow-up is cast only when spell-then-move beats the plain move (aiFreeFollowUp).
    • Evaluation (evaluate): material (PIECE_SCORE; the custom pieces are set between their shop price and their measured reach on an empty board: Camel 230, Cricket 140, Centaur 650, Rat 450, Fake Knight 420, Cat 600, Shooter 650, Amazon 1300), piece-square tables (hand-tuned 8x8 tables for the classic pieces; a generated centre/advance/back-rank table for every other piece and board size, buildGenericPst), mobility, king-zone pressure (squares hit around each king, weighted up sharply by the number of attacking pieces, kingDanger; its weight AI_PARAMS.kingAttack is 0 by default because it did not help in self-play), king shelter, doubled/isolated and passed pawns, bishop pair, rooks/queens on open files, endgame king activity and king hunting, control effects (slept −30%, confused −22%, cursed −12%, shielded +10%), loot markers, bounties, and money at AI_PARAMS.money (50) centipawns per $ up to $12, half that beyond (moneyValue) — roughly what money buys in the shop, less the turn spent buying.
    • The search mirrors the live capture economy: applyMove credits half the victim’s value, the bounty payout, Plunder +$2, Thief’s $1 steal, golden +$1, platinum +$3 and the $1 cursed-mover toll (applyMoveIncome), and carries Piratify/Thief/Curse/Shield markers with a moving piece.
    • Repetition: there is no repetition draw, so a root move that recreates a position the AI already produced costs repPenalty (25) per earlier occurrence; this stops aimless shuffling.
    • Self-play: await aiSelfPlay({games, levelA, levelB, paramsA, paramsB, maxPlies, size}) in the browser console plays AI-vs-AI games without rendering (AI_HEADLESS), alternating colours, and reports wins per configuration (games reaching maxPlies are adjudicated at ±300 or drawn). It is how the weights in AI_DEFAULT_PARAMS are checked (results are noted next to them); a fresh game replaces the current one afterwards.
    • Safety nets: if its planned move can't be applied (e.g. a piece became curse-frozen), it plays any legal move (aiFallbackMove); if it has no moves at all it ends the game as a stalemate draw.

    All game code lives in money-chess.html, in two <script> blocks: aiEngine (the AI: evaluation, search and decisions; it must not touch the DOM at load time because it also runs in a Web Worker, see §12) followed by mainScript (rules, UI, networking).     15. Known issues, bugs & inconsistencies (to fix later)

    1. Castling is effectively 8x8-only. makeBackRank() places a Rook on an edge file only for sides of the board where the generated rank puts one there (king-side on 8x8 & 15x15-left (queen-side), nothing elsewhere), and castle detection requires the edge square to be a Rook. In practice full, both-sides castling only exists on the 8x8 board; the other sizes silently never offer it.
    2. Chess 960 back rank is not a shuffled permutation. The 960 commit message says "the same pieces shuffled into different positions", but makeRandomBackRank() draws each non-king file independently from the full roster (duplicates possible, pieces can appear 0 times, and pawns can be placed on the back rank). Clarify intent or change to a true permutation.
    3. Forced self-stalemate under Echo: Echo's no-capture filter (noCapture) can leave a player with zero legal moves; endTurnChecks() then records an instant "Stalemate â€” Draw". A player could be forced into a draw purely by having only capturing moves. Reconsider.
    4. squareAttacked ignores Walls and sleeping attackers. Movement stops at a Wall (pseudoMoves) but the attack test still sees a slider's line through it, and a sleeping piece (which cannot capture next turn) still counts as attacking. So "Check!", castling legality and the AI's king-safety tests can be too cautious around Walls and Sleep.
